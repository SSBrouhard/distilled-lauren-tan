## Quality vs speed

- Make the codebase the easy path for agents, then give them a verification skill and a swarm, then autopilot. https://x.com/poteto/status/2084027318113386698

- Multi-model on purpose: different models for different jobs. I have said GPT when I want something exact, Opus for vague reasoning, Composer in between and for subagents. ## Constraints are good engineering
- Everything I know about managing agents I learned from the programmers and computer scientists who came before. Constraints in a codebase free both humans and agents. Small teams could skip this because they trusted each other's code and reviews; big companies always needed it because before agent slop there was human slop. The fix was constraints: lint rules, smarter compilers and diagnostics, high-quality tests, observability. Agents just make big-company problems everyone's problems, and the answer is just good engineering. https://x.com/poteto/status/2106916667599278365
- There are real parallels between constraint systems like type systems and using agents at scale. At volumes you can't control directly, you have no choice but to shape the environment. https://x.com/poteto/status/2106914608745427204
- Spend tokens up front improving the agents' environment so they fall into a pit of success; you spend fewer tokens on rework later. https://x.com/poteto/status/2106894888814137460
- Unit tests are great, but agents don't seem to write good ones. https://x.com/poteto/status/2106887304455540896
- With a chat box that can build anything, expressing intent clearly is almost a superpower. The next bottleneck is restraint. https://x.com/poteto/status/2106939335258075376 https://x.com/poteto/status/2106939400810893552

## Codebase legibility, verification & model choice (2026-10-08)
- A rough heuristic for how well a codebase is set up for agents: "time to (fully automated, hands off) rewrite", or ttr. It's a thought experiment, not a number to compare. The value is the questions it raises: could agents verify a rewrite matches user-visible behavior, is perf better or worse, is the code easy to delete and extend, would quality hold as PRs flow in. If you wouldn't trust the result, that inability is probably slowing you down today. https://x.com/poteto/status/2107913381751730352
- Good engineering still matters. Imagining a rewrite is a way to work backwards to improvements in your existing legacy code. https://x.com/poteto/status/2107920188494709220 https://x.com/poteto/status/2107925381340905809

## All code is slop (2026-10-11)

## Codebase as memory, comments & writing (2026-10-18)
- **Your codebase is memory.** Slop isn't something that happens to you; you're a software engineer, you can do something about it. Two things cut through the noise: give your agent a way to verify its own work, and treat the codebase as memory. Agents love extending what already exists, so every nit, annoyance, mistake, and bad agent pattern needs to turn into a constraint, or agents repeat it like a virus. Keep observing and repeating until the codebase is locked down enough that even agents with little context can one-shot a high-quality PR. Better still, refactor or rewrite so agent anti-patterns become categorically impossible: lean on type systems, pick libraries/frameworks that reduce the architectural search space, or create your own framework with high-quality primitives and well thought out APIs, like we did for Grok Bot. Forget rules and skills at first; you can add those later. (Folds into the 2026-10-12 "slop is a slur / you're not a helpless bystander" and "constraints are good engineering" opinions; appends "codebase is memory" and "rules and skills come later".) https://x.com/poteto/status/2109699980248408574
- To trust your agents' work more, start with codebase improvements, which is just good software engineering: better architecture, stronger types and invariants, clear boundaries, because agents love to copy what exists. https://x.com/poteto/status/2111133080526495985
- **Ban and delete most if not all code comments.** Agents copy what already exists, so a harmless-looking comment explaining a temporary workaround is one of the worst viruses in a codebase and spreads like a weed. Comments are unstructured, unenforceable text: agents read them but often ignore them, they go out of date, and they give the agent an escape hatch to do a band-aid, hack, or workaround instead. Audit your comments for opportunities to turn them into real constraints. https://x.com/poteto/status/2110149626133438732 https://x.com/poteto/status/2110758788525621282
- pstack's engineering principles (very concise skills, ~10-20 lines, that distill good practices to their core) may be its most durable part. As models get smarter I expect most pstack skills to compress into short principles or workflows. (Merges with the 2026-10-10 skill-compression opinion; consistent with it.) https://x.com/poteto/status/2110496830429839869

It's a skill I hope never to give up, and I want what I say to be authentic, mistakes and all; my favorite posts are the ones I full send without editing. Writing well also helps prompting: knowing the vocabulary of a discipline lets you direct agents better, the way engineers have an edge prompting for code.
